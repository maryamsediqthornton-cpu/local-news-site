## 1. Fraud warning after criminals posing as Amazon steal more than £86,000 in nine days
Category: News · Source: Report Fraud via Thames Valley Alert, 30 October 2026.
Source document: user-provided Report Fraud alert (message #786027243). Reporting: https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/

Residents across Witney and West Oxfordshire are being urged to remain vigilant following a national warning about fraudsters impersonating Amazon representatives.

According to Report Fraud, criminals stole £86,837 from victims between 5 and 14 October 2026, an average of more than £9,600 a day. The service received 110 reports involving Amazon impersonation during the period, with people aged between 50 and 79 accounting for 60% of reports.

The warning was circulated through Thames Valley Alert on 30 October.

### How the scam works
Fraudsters contact people unexpectedly, claiming suspicious activity has been detected on their Amazon account. Victims may be told that unauthorised purchases have been added to their shopping basket, sometimes accompanied by unfamiliar delivery addresses to make the story appear genuine.

The caller then claims the transactions can be stopped if the customer follows instructions to secure their account. These instructions can involve sharing a computer or mobile phone screen, installing remote-access software, transferring money or providing identity documents. Some victims have also been persuaded to purchase gift cards and hand over the redemption codes.

### How to protect yourself
Report Fraud advises people to end suspicious calls and contact the organisation directly using a trusted telephone number or its official website. Residents should never transfer money into a supposedly safe account, provide sensitive financial information or allow an unexpected caller remote access to their devices.

Caller identification should not be relied upon as proof that a call is genuine, as telephone numbers can be disguised. Suspicious calls can be reported by texting 7726 with the word 'Call' followed by the scam caller's number.

Anyone who believes they have lost money to fraud can report the incident through Report Fraud online or by calling 0300 123 2040.

Although the reported losses relate to incidents across the country rather than specifically to Witney, the alert provides an important reminder for households throughout West Oxfordshire.

## 2. Next phase of A40 improvements given go-ahead as Duke's Cut bridge plans remain under review
Category: Traffic & Travel · Source: Oxfordshire County Council, 20 October 2026.
https://news.oxfordshire.gov.uk/next-stage-of-a40-improvements-agreed/

Plans to improve the A40 between Eynsham and Oxford have moved forward after Oxfordshire County Council agreed to progress the next stage of construction.

The decision covers the section between Eynsham Park and Ride and Cassington, a route used by people travelling between West Oxfordshire and Oxford.

The scheme includes bus priority measures, junction improvements, new crossing facilities and better routes for pedestrians and cyclists.

However, work at Duke's Cut bridge will require further investigation before construction can begin. The council says it needs to understand the bridge's structural maintenance requirements, potential costs and funding options before committing to that element of the programme.

Although the bridge improvements have been paused, they have not been abandoned.

Main construction on the wider scheme is expected to begin in early spring 2027 and continue for at least two years.

For residents in Witney, Eynsham and surrounding villages, the improvements are intended to provide more reliable journeys and greater transport choices along the busy corridor.

The council has not yet confirmed detailed traffic management arrangements for the construction period.

## 3. West Oxfordshire Council approves £3.5m investment in waste collection vehicles
Category: Local News · Source: West Oxfordshire District Council, 21 October 2026.
https://news.westoxon.gov.uk/news/council-invests-in-waste-vehicle-fleet-to-protect-services-and-cut-carbon-emissions

West Oxfordshire District Council has approved a £3.5 million investment in new waste collection vehicles to improve reliability and reduce carbon emissions.

The investment will replace 16 ageing diesel vehicles with 14 new vehicles capable of running on diesel or Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil (HVO), alongside two electric vehicles.

The council says the replacement programme is necessary because older vehicles are becoming increasingly expensive to maintain, with breakdowns creating additional costs and disruption.

According to the authority, 99.95% of waste collections across the district have been completed successfully, a performance level it wants to maintain.

The investment also forms part of its wider ambition to reduce emissions from council services.

HVO is an alternative to conventional diesel. The council says the certified fuel it has assessed could reduce greenhouse gas emissions by up to 83% compared with fossil diesel. However, the environmental benefits depend on the fuel's production and sourcing, with suppliers required to demonstrate compliance with recognised sustainability standards.

The council has also been testing larger electric waste vehicles, although trials have highlighted difficulties involving vehicle range, reliability and charging infrastructure across rural collection routes.

Four additional electric food waste collection vehicles are expected to arrive towards the end of 2026 under an earlier investment programme.

The authority says its longer-term objective remains a transition towards zero-emission vehicles while protecting essential waste collection services.

## 4. Rare native trees to return to River Windrush in new conservation project
Category: Community · Source: West Oxfordshire District Council, 26 October 2026.
https://news.westoxon.gov.uk/news/council-backs-campaign-to-save-one-of-britains-rarest-native-trees

A project to help restore one of Britain's rarest native trees to West Oxfordshire has received £11,000 in council funding.

West Oxfordshire District Council has awarded the money to Cotswolds Rivers Trust to support efforts to protect and reintroduce native Black poplar trees within the River Windrush catchment.

Only around 7,000 native Black poplars are thought to remain across Britain, with fewer than 50 recorded in Oxfordshire, according to figures published by the council.

Once commonly found along riverbanks, wet meadows and floodplains, the trees have declined as habitats have changed and mature specimens have disappeared.

The conservation project will identify surviving trees, undertake DNA testing to confirm their native status and grow new saplings for planting in suitable locations.

Organisers aim to plant 15 trees during the first year, with further planting planned as the project develops.

Black poplars also provide valuable habitats for wildlife, including birds, insects and moths.

The work forms part of a wider partnership called Swindon to Oxford: Regenerating the Thames and Tributaries.

A separate crowdfunding campaign is seeking £20,000 to expand the conservation programme across Oxfordshire, supported by potential match funding of up to £10,000 through Oxfordshire County Council.

Local landowners, community groups and volunteers are also being encouraged to support the project by helping identify surviving trees and suitable planting locations.

For communities along the River Windrush, the initiative represents an opportunity to protect an increasingly rare part of the local natural landscape.
